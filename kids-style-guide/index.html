01 · Foundations

Play with purpose.

Every choice should feel lively enough for kids and trustworthy enough for grown-ups. The system is expressive, never chaotic; tactile, never toy-like.

1

Clear first

Big type, plain words, and obvious actions help everyone know what to do next.

2

Joy in the edges

Color, tilt, texture, and bounce add delight without getting in the way.

3

Made to last

Simple shapes and sturdy patterns keep the experience timeless and dependable.

02 · Color

03 · Typography

Round, loud, readable.

Rounded display type carries personality. A calm humanist sans handles longer copy. Both stacks use familiar system fonts for speed and broad device support.

05 · Voice & motion

Encourage the next try.

Sound upbeat, direct, and respectful. Motion should mimic a block settling into place: quick, physical, and complete.

Build this way

  • Say “Let’s try another way.”
  • Celebrate effort and discovery.
  • Use movement to confirm an action.
  • Honor reduced-motion preferences.

Leave this in the box

  • “Oops! You did it wrong.”
  • Baby talk or exaggerated slang.
  • Endless bouncing or spinning.
  • Color as the only status signal.
